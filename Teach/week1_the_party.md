## Part 1: The Call [image=images/nina.png]

It is 7:40 PM on Monday, December 18th. Snow is falling over Chicago.

Sergeant Nina Reyes of the Chicago Police Department is sitting in her car outside the precinct when her phone rings. A young man — Alex Martin, 28, a PhD student — has been found unconscious in his apartment on South Woodlawn Avenue. Head injury. Ambulance on its way.

Nina drives fast.

When she arrives, the paramedics are already there. She shows her badge and follows them inside. In the bathroom of a small third-floor apartment, a young man is lying on the floor. He is breathing, but he does not respond. There is a serious wound on the back of his head. The bathroom tap is running.

The paramedics lift Alex onto a stretcher. "He's stable," one of them tells Nina. "We're taking him now."

Nina nods. She stays behind.
### The Card [image=images/clue3.png]
In the hallway outside the bathroom, she crouches down. On the floor, near the bathroom door, there is a small card. She reads it without touching it.

*"Pay up or die!"*

### The Weapon [image=images/clue2.png]
She stands up and looks around. On the bathroom floor, half under the sink, she can see a heavy gold-coloured object — a trophy of some kind, about thirty centimetres tall. There is blood on it.

Near the base of the toilet, something small catches her eye. She crouches down again. It is a tiny plastic cap — the kind that covers the end of a USB drive. An unusual shape: it looks like the nose of a small plane, with a tiny propeller. It could have been there for days. It could mean nothing. But it is strange to find in a bathroom.

She takes out her notebook.

Three people are waiting in the living room.

## Part 2: Three Suspects

Nina looks at the three people in the living room. A young woman with red hair and a pink sweater is standing by the wall, her arms folded tightly across her chest. A tall man in a grey shirt stands behind the sofa, leaning on the back of it, looking at Nina. A third man — brown hair, glasses, an olive-green shirt — is sitting on the sofa, staring at his phone.

Nina closes her notebook.

"Nobody leaves," she says. "I'm going to speak to each of you separately. Let's start with you." She points to the woman by the wall.

---

**FIONA**

Her name is Fiona McAllister, second-year Biology student. She is Alex's girlfriend.

"I arrived at seven o'clock," she says. "I have a key — Alex gave it to me last month. The apartment was dark. I called his name, but he didn't answer. His coat was on the chair by the door, so I knew he was home. I could hear water running in the bathroom. I knocked. No answer. I tried calling his phone." She pauses. "I could hear it ringing in the bedroom."

"Then what?"

"Then Tim and Ronnie arrived. That was around five past seven. Ronnie buzzed the intercom and I let them in. About five minutes later I went back to the bathroom and knocked again. Nothing. So I opened the door."

Nina writes. "And before you arrived here — where were you this evening?"

"At the library until six. Then I drove straight here."

"The trophy we found in the bathroom — do you recognise it?"

Fiona nods slowly. "It's from their office at the university, I've seen it there. It's been on the shelf forever — some old award, I think." She frowns. "I don't know what it was doing here."

Nina looks at her. "Is there anything else that seemed unusual to you this evening? Or recently?"

Fiona is quiet for a moment. "Alex has been... different lately. More closed off. He doesn't talk to me much about his work, which is nothing new, but recently it's felt like something else — like he's been carrying something. And tonight —" she looks around the room — "I mean, look at this group. Tim's his office mate, Ronnie's practically a stranger. These aren't his close friends. Alex doesn't really have close friends. I always thought that was sad."

**TIM**

Tim Kane. PhD student in Finance. He shares an office with Alex at the university. He looks almost as shaken as Fiona. He speaks fast, and it seems like he is trying not to cry.

"When did you arrive?"

"With Ronnie. Five past seven — well, around then. He buzzed the intercom, Fiona let us in." He rubs his face. "Alex wasn't there — we thought maybe he'd stepped out, or — I don't know. Then Fiona found him —" He stops.

"Take your time," Nina says. "Where were you earlier this evening?"

"I left the university around six. Six, six-fifteen, somewhere around there. I had to — I needed to sort some things out. Personal stuff." He pauses. "I was actually here a bit before — I mean, I arrived at five past seven, but I'd been driving around before that. Parking here is always a nightmare, so I was circling for a while."

Nina looks up from her notebook. "Here a bit before — before what?"

Tim blinks. "Before — before Ronnie. I meant before Ronnie arrived. We got there at the same time, five past seven."

"The card we found outside the bathroom," Nina asks. "'Pay up or die.' Have you seen it before?"

"Yes — no — I mean, yes." He exhales. "Someone left something like that on my desk at work. A few days ago. I don't know who. I assumed it was some stupid joke." He glances up. "Is it — do you think the same person could have attacked Alex?"

## Part 3: A New Witness

At 10:00 PM, the intercom buzzes.

Nobody is expecting anyone. Nina looks at the others — Fiona straightens in her chair, her eyes sharp. Nina walks to the intercom and presses the button.

"Hello?" A woman's voice. "Is Alex there?"

"This is Sergeant Reyes, Chicago PD. Come up, please."

A pause. Then the door clicks open downstairs.

A minute later, a well-dressed woman with dark hair appears in the doorway. She takes in the room — the police tape on the bathroom door, Nina's badge, the three faces looking at her — and something in her expression tightens. She is holding her bag very tightly.

"I'm Pauline Thompson," she says. Her voice is steady, but it costs her something. "I was supposed to meet Alex tonight. What happened? Is he—"

"He's in hospital," Nina says. "He's alive."

Pauline closes her eyes for a moment. "Thank God."

From across the room, Fiona is staring at her. "Who are you?"

Pauline looks at her. "A friend of Alex's. We work together."

"He never mentioned you."

"We keep our work separate from — other things." She says it without expression.

Fiona's jaw tightens. "You were here earlier, weren't you. Before any of us arrived. He met you here and didn't tell me."

A silence. Pauline doesn't answer. She looks at Nina instead.

Nina steps in. "We'll continue this separately." She turns to Fiona. "I'll come back to you." Then to Pauline: "This way."

---

Alone with Nina, Pauline sits down and takes a breath.

"You're Alex's business partner?" Nina asks.

"Yes. We've been working together for two years. He developed a financial formula. I use it for trading."

"And tonight?"

"We had an argument — earlier this evening. I've been asking him for weeks to explain the formula to me properly. I can't keep using it without understanding how it works. But he keeps making excuses and putting it off. Tonight was the same." She pauses. "He sent me to his university office to collect a USB drive. The formula is on it. I brought it to him, gave him back his keys, and left. That was around twenty to seven."

"You came to his apartment before the party?"

"Yes. When I arrived, one of his guest was already there — Alex's office mate, his name is Tim, I think. Alex came downstairs to speak to me privately; we talked in the stairwell." She pauses. "After that I went to the university. When I came back at around twenty to seven, Tim was gone."

"And on your way back here — did you notice anything outside?"

"A blue car, parked right at the entrance. The spot had been empty when I arrived. Someone was sitting inside."

## Part 4: The Confrontation

Nina sits down opposite Tim.

"Pauline Thompson arrived here at six o'clock this evening," she says. "You were already in the apartment. She confirms that. She left for the university shortly after — and when she came back at twenty to seven, you were gone. On her way back, she noticed a blue car parked outside that hadn't been there before. Someone sitting inside."

Tim does not answer and doesn't raise his eyes.

"Your car is at the corner right now. The keys are locked inside — you told Ronnie that yourself. And, by the way, you didn't tell me. Why?"

Tim looks up for the first time. "I — yes, okay, I did drive here earlier. I came to talk to Alex about something. But then I — I changed my mind, I didn't want to bother him before the party, so I left again. I drove around for a bit. That's why the car was there. That's all."

"And the keys?"

"I got out in a hurry and — it just — it locked by itself. I didn't notice. And it's not a crime to lock your keys in your car, is it?"

"It is not," - Nina says quietly.
"The trophy in the bathroom came from your shared office at the university. Fiona McAllister recognised it — she said it's been on the shelf there for years."

Tim swallows. "Pauline was in that office this evening. You said so yourself. She was there too."

Nina looks at him steadily. "Pauline drove here at ten o'clock and buzzed the intercom. When she heard there was a police officer inside, she could have said she had the wrong address and walked away. Instead she came up and answered every question." She pauses. "Why would she do that if she had something to hide?"

Tim opens his mouth. Nothing comes out. Then, desperately: "And even if I wanted to — how would I have gotten in? I can't just walk into someone's apartment."

From across the room, Fiona's voice cuts in, sharp and flat. "He has the building entry code — Alex gave it to everyone he had invited. And the apartment door was unlocked. I found it that way when I arrived."

Nina leans forward. "You were in this building before the party. You had the entry code. You had the trophy. And you had twenty-five minutes alone with Alex that you have not mentioned once."

Nina leans forward slightly. "Tim. What happened in that apartment?"

For a long moment, the room is completely silent.
"I didn't mean to hurt him badly." Tim's voice breaks. "I just needed the drive. I owe Ronnie a lot of money — from investments that went wrong. I couldn't pay him back. Then this evening I found out Alex had a formula worth a fortune. I thought — if I could get the drive, I could sell the formula. I could pay everything back."

He stops.

"I went in while he was alone. He was in the bathroom. I hit him from behind. He fell and hit the sink. There was so much blood — I panicked. I ran." He pauses. "I took the drive. And I dropped Ronnie's card there, to — I don't know. I wasn't thinking clearly."

Nina stands up.

"Tim Kane, you are under arrest."

Tim reaches into his pocket and places a small USB drive on the coffee table. Without its cap, it looks like a tiny plane that has lost its nose.

She picks it up. It is light. A little silly-looking.

Whatever Alex had spent years building — the formula that Pauline traded with, that Tim had been willing to go this far for — was on this ridiculous little object in her hand. Nina turns it over once, then slips it into an evidence bag.

She does not know what is on it. Not yet.
