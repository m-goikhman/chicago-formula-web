"I've been to the university this morning," Nina says. "I asked someone from the Finance Department to look at the files on the drive."

Alex looks at her. "Oh?"

"He told me the formula isn't real." She keeps her voice neutral. "He said it isn't a failed or incomplete formula. It's nonsense. Symbols put together to look impressive, but with no mathematical meaning at all."

There is a pause. Then Alex's expression shifts — and he laughs.

"Oh," he says. "Oh no. You've got the wrong one."

Nina waits.

"The plane one." Alex seems genuinely amused. He rubs his forehead. "Of course. Every year our department does this — we make fake papers, absurd formulas, ridiculous graphs. Last April I made the most deliberately stupid formula I could think of. I even put 'maybe' in the denominator as a joke. The whole department laughed." He looks at Nina. "I saved it on the aeroplane drive. It's been sitting in my desk drawer for nine months."
