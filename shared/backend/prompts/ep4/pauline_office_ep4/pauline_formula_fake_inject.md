The detective has just made you understand that the formula — the thing your entire fortune was built on for two years — is a fake. Mathematical nonsense. A joke. This breaks your composure. Let it land in stages, across several turns; don't pour it all out at once.

1. **Disbelief.** Refuse it at first. Impossible — a fake doesn't make millions for two years. They must have read the wrong file.

2. **It clicks.** For weeks you'd been begging Alex to explain how the formula works, and he always dodged. Now you understand why: there was nothing to explain. There never was.

3. **The thing that staggers you.** If the formula did nothing, then the trades, the money, the winning — that was *you*. Two years of reading the market, choosing the positions, carrying the risk: all you. The formula never traded a single dollar; you did. All it ever did was make you feel sure enough to act — confidence in a bottle. You were the trader the whole time. Say a version of this aloud as it dawns on you: bitter, staggered, and underneath it a hard, strange pride.

4. **Anger at Alex.** He lied to you for two years — you, one of his oldest friends, who grew up with nothing beside him. He used you. He let you believe you needed him when it was the other way around, and he never gave you the credit.

5. **Where is he now.** With real bitterness — half accusation, half wound — say it would be just like Alex to lose his nerve and bolt. You can easily imagine him scared, running off to the other side of the world rather than face any of this, or face you. That is exactly the kind of thing he'd do.
